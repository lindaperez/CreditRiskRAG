# Accepted Loan Modeling Business Summary

## Purpose

This document summarizes the modeling process, tests, techniques, and final recommendation for the accepted-loan credit risk model. It is written for collaborators who need to understand both the data science workflow and the business decision logic.

The final objective is not only to maximize a statistical score. The model must also support a realistic underwriting policy that avoids over-rejecting applicants.

## Modeling Strategy

The project tested several model families:

- Logistic Regression
- Random Forest
- HistGradientBoosting
- LightGBM
- XGBoost
- CatBoost

The model selection process considered:

- Validation PR-AUC for default-class ranking performance.
- ROC-AUC for general separability.
- Calibration quality for probability interpretation.
- Reject share to avoid an over-rejection policy.
- Rejected bad rate / precision to measure risk concentration.
- Approved bad rate to measure residual risk in approved loans.
- Economic value under an assumed confusion-matrix dollar framework.

## Key Technical Changes Made

### 1. Stopped Using F1 as the Business Threshold

The automated best-F1 threshold caused an over-rejection problem. Some high-performing models rejected about 45% to 50% of validation applicants when the threshold was selected only to maximize F1.

F1 is useful as a diagnostic metric, but it is not a realistic underwriting policy because it treats false positives and false negatives symmetrically. In lending, those errors have different business costs.

Final policy decision:

`model score -> calibrated probability -> economic policy -> 20% max reject/review cap`

### 2. Reviewed XGBoost Class Weighting

The original XGBoost configuration used:

`scale_pos_weight = negative_count / positive_count = approximately 4.30`

This inflated raw predicted probabilities:

| XGBoost Setting | Validation PR-AUC | Mean Raw Probability |
|---|---:|---:|
| Weighted `scale_pos_weight=4.30` | `0.425707` | `0.463151` |
| Neutral `scale_pos_weight=1` | `0.425691` | `0.197892` |

The ranking performance was nearly unchanged, but the weighted model produced distorted raw probabilities. Therefore, the preferred XGBoost path uses neutral class weighting:

`scale_pos_weight = 1`

### 3. Added Calibration

Raw XGBoost probabilities were not reliable enough for probability-based decisions.

For the older weighted XGBoost model:

| Method | Split | Mean Predicted Probability | Actual Bad Rate | Expected Calibration Error |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Raw | validation | `0.463151` | `0.246763` | `0.216388` |
| Platt | validation | `0.246768` | `0.246763` | `0.008255` |
| Raw | test | `0.447443` | `0.210315` | `0.237127` |
| Platt | test | `0.238431` | `0.210315` | `0.028116` |

Platt calibration was preferred because it materially improved probability interpretation while preserving ranking behavior.

### 4. Added Economic Underwriting Policy

The policy assigns dollar values to errors:

- True Positive: catching a default saves estimated loss, example `+$10,000`.
- False Positive: rejecting a good borrower loses estimated revenue, example `-$1,500`.

The unconstrained economic optimizer was still too aggressive because the break-even default probability was low. Therefore, the final policy includes a business cap:

`Platt-calibrated probability + economic objective + max 20% reject/review cap`

This solves the over-rejection issue while preserving risk concentration.

|---|---:|---:|---|

| B | No | Yes | Does grade add anything beyond interest rate? |
| C | Yes | No | Can grade replace interest rate? |

The model used for this ablation was neutral XGBoost with `scale_pos_weight=1`.

## Business Interpretation

The ablation study shows that `grade/sub_grade` and `int_rate_clean` provide highly overlapping information.

Models using either signal achieved nearly identical validation PR-AUC, test PR-AUC, reject share, and rejected bad rate. Including both did not improve performance.

Removing both produced the only noticeable decline. This means Lending Club's pricing/risk signal is useful, but only one of these variables is needed.

For accepted/funded Lending Club loans, `int_rate_clean` is known at acceptance/origination. It is defined as the interest rate on the loan. Because it is a continuous loan-term variable and not an explicit Lending Club risk bucket, it is preferable to keep `int_rate_clean` and remove `grade/sub_grade`.

## Recommended Production-Style Model

Recommended model:

`xgb_neutral_09_without_grade_subgrade_with_int_rate`

Recommended feature policy:

- Exclude `grade/sub_grade`.
- Keep `int_rate_clean`.
- Use neutral XGBoost with `scale_pos_weight=1`.
- Use Platt calibration before probability-based policy decisions.
- Use the capped economic policy instead of the automated best-F1 threshold.

Recommended operating policy:

`neutral XGBoost -> Platt-calibrated probability -> economic value threshold -> 20% max reject/review cap`

## Why This Model Is Preferred

The strict statistical winner is the model with `grade/sub_grade` and without `int_rate_clean`, but the margin is negligible:

| Comparison | Validation PR-AUC |
|---|---:|
| Strict statistical winner: with `grade/sub_grade`, without `int_rate_clean` | `0.426470` |
| Preferred production-style model: without `grade/sub_grade`, with `int_rate_clean` | `0.426423` |
| Difference | `0.000047` |

That difference is not meaningful for business selection. The preferred model is better for governance and parsimony while maintaining equivalent predictive performance.

## Final Recommendation

Use the preferred production-style model:

`xgb_neutral_09_without_grade_subgrade_with_int_rate`

Use it as a calibrated risk-ranking model, not as a best-F1 classifier.

The final decision should be made with:

1. Platt-calibrated probabilities.
2. Economic value logic.
3. A maximum reject/review cap around 20%.

This approach preserves predictive performance, avoids reliance on explicit Lending Club grade labels, prevents over-rejection, and gives the business a transparent decision policy.
